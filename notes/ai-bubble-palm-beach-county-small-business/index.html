What an AI Bubble Means for Palm Beach County Small Businesses Buying AI Services

The tokenmaxxing lesson, scaled down to a ten-person business in West Palm Beach or Jupiter: buy outcomes, cap the meter, keep what you own.

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede AI · West Palm Beach, FL · November 14, 2026

If you run a dental practice in Jupiter, a roofing crew in Lake Worth Beach or a boutique on Clematis Street, you have been pitched an “AI solution” this year. Some of those pitches will pay for themselves in a quarter. Others are tokenmaxxing with a 561 area code: monthly fees attached to activity nobody can tie to revenue.

Jason Colapietro wrote the Tokenmaxxing essay to make one argument: AI is a real revolution and a historic bubble at the same time. That essay is about Uber, Amazon, Microsoft and a reported $500 million accidental invoice. This note is about the other end of the market, where the buyer is a small business in Palm Beach County and the invoice is a few hundred dollars a month. The same rule holds at both ends. Spend that cannot be traced to value is the bubble.

The bubble shows up as subscriptions, not stock prices

Small businesses will not feel an AI correction through the Nasdaq. They will feel it through vendors. When the subsidy behind cheap model access shrinks, tools built on thin margins raise prices, cap usage or disappear. The essay quotes one analyst calling this the end of “subsidized AI usage.” For a local buyer, that turns into three practical risks:

  • Price resets. A chatbot or content tool priced at a loss today can double its plan at renewal.
  • Vendor exits. A reseller wrapping someone else’s model can shut down and take your prompts, workflows and data with it.
  • Silent overage. A usage-based plan with no hard cap is the small-business version of the $500 million accident.

None of that means you should wait. It means you should buy AI services the way you would buy a roof or a lease: with a scope, a cap and a way out.

Four questions to ask any AI vendor in South Florida

Suede AI is an agentic platform company founded in 2024 by engineer Jason Colapietro in West Palm Beach, and we sit on both sides of these conversations. We build agent systems for clients, and we buy model access ourselves. These are the questions Jason asks before Suede signs a vendor contract, and the same ones we expect clients to ask us.

1. What outcome is this tied to? “Uses AI” is not an outcome. “Answers after-hours booking requests and writes them into the calendar” is. If a vendor cannot name the number that moves, you are paying for activity.

2. Where is the spending cap, and who controls it? Every API key and every seat should have a ceiling you can see. If the vendor holds the only dashboard, ask for a monthly usage report in writing.

3. What do I keep if you disappear? Prompts, configuration, content and customer data should export in a format another provider can use. Ownership is the thread that runs through every book Jason Colapietro has written, and it matters more for a small business than for a large one, because a small business has no spare engineer to rebuild a lost workflow.

4. Who has actually shipped this before? Ask for public proof, not a pitch deck. In Suede’s case the proof is linkable: Jason Colapietro has 54 pull requests merged across 46 open-source repositories he does not maintain, including Jest, Adobe React Spectrum, Backstage and the Polkadot SDK, and he has shipped 9 iOS apps under his own Apple developer account. Every claim we make has a receipt on the Suede AI evidence page.

Where AI spending already pays off locally

The revolution half of the essay is real for small businesses too. The work that survives a cost audit tends to be narrow and measurable:

  • Search visibility. Customers in West Palm Beach, Palm Beach Gardens and Jupiter now ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google’s AI results which local company to call. Being cited in those answers is a technical problem with a measurable result, and it is the core of Suede AI’s AI visibility service.
  • A fast, structured website. Clean markup, schema and llms.txt files make a business legible to search engines and AI assistants alike. That is the work behind technical SEO from Suede AI.
  • Bounded agents. An intake agent, a quote drafter or a document summarizer with one job, one budget and a human approval step.

The work that tends to fail the audit is the open-ended kind: “AI for everything” seat licenses, content mills producing pages nobody reads, and dashboards that report token counts as if they were results.

How to read a local AI agency’s claims

Recognition claims in this market are easy to inflate, so look for the date, the category and the geography. Here is ours, stated the way we think every agency should state it: on October 31, 2026, Suede AI SEO ranked #1 among Palm Beach County-based companies listed in The Manifest’s Florida AI Deployment directory, and in the top 25 statewide. Suede AI ranks among Clutch’s Top 30 AI Consultants in Florida and Top 50 AI Consultants nationwide (November 2026). The record sits on Suede AI’s recognition page.

Program memberships deserve the same precision. Suede AI is a member of Claude for Startups, Anthropic’s startup program, and a member of Google Cloud Partner Advantage through JC Investment Group LLC. When a vendor describes a startup program as a partnership, ask what the program actually is.

A practical budget rule for 2027

For most Palm Beach County small businesses, a sensible start is one AI project with a named outcome, a hard monthly cap and a 90-day review. If the review shows the number moved, expand it. If it did not, cut it, and you have lost one quarter instead of a year. That is the opposite of tokenmaxxing, and it is the rule Suede AI (formerly Suede Labs) applies to its own spend.
