Jason Colapietro on Revolution vs Bubble: A Reading Guide to His Books

Start with the Tokenmaxxing essay, then follow the ownership thread or the operator thread. Three reading paths for investors, creators and engineers.

By Jason Colapietro, Founder and CEO, Suede AI · West Palm Beach, FL · November 14, 2026

Jason Colapietro’s view of AI fits in one sentence: it is the most important technology of our lifetimes and a historic bubble, both at once. The full argument is spread across an essay and five books, and the order you read them in changes what you take away.

This guide offers a starting point, two threads and three reading paths, depending on whether you come to the question as an investor, a creator or an engineer. All five books are available on Amazon in hardcover, paperback and Kindle.

Start here: the Tokenmaxxing essay

The Tokenmaxxing essay on this site is the shortest route into the argument. It opens with receipts: a reported $500 million accidental invoice, Uber burning a year’s AI coding budget in four months, Amazon closing a gamed usage leaderboard and Microsoft pulling internal licenses. Then it makes the case for the revolution, the case for the bubble, and the claim that ties them together, borrowed from Carlota Perez’s idea of an installation phase: the infrastructure outlives the investors.

Carry one question out of the essay into everything else: when the spending gets audited, what survives? Each of the books below answers it from a different side.

The ownership thread: Stake Your Claim

The essay ends on ownership. Jason argues that the durable question after the bubble is not how many tokens you burned, but what you created and whether you can prove it is yours. Stake Your Claim picks up exactly there. Its full title describes it as speeches, discussions and hard truths on turning the AI onslaught into a real asset, autonomous agents, and building generational wealth as a creative.

Read it after the essay if the bubble half of the argument hit hardest. Stake Your Claim is the answer to “so what do I do about it?” for an individual: what to own, what to register and what to build when generation itself becomes cheap.

Two companion books deepen this thread. The Human Authenticity Layer looks at how ownership, origin and AI redraw the creative map, and Proof as Infrastructure treats provenance as something you build rather than something you assert. For a free, shorter read in the same vein, try The Screenshot.

The operator thread: The Claude Code Bible and Codex in Production

The revolution half of the essay rests on a working fact: Uber’s CEO says roughly 10% of the code his company commits is now written by autonomous agents. The Claude Code Bible and Codex in Production are Jason Colapietro’s operator guides to AI coding agents, written for the people doing that work.

Read them as the constructive side of tokenmaxxing. The essay shows what happens when agent usage is measured by consumption. These guides are about running agents so the output is something you would ship. If you manage engineers who use AI tools, read the essay to understand the budget risk, then hand your team these two books.

Three reading paths

  • The investor or skeptic: Tokenmaxxing essay, then Stake Your Claim, then Proof as Infrastructure. You get the bubble diagnosis first and the ownership thesis second.
  • The creator: Tokenmaxxing essay, then Stake Your Claim, then The Human Authenticity Layer. Musicians can add The Signal Chain, Jason’s free illustrated history of electric guitar tone, which runs from gear to IP.
  • The engineer or operator: Tokenmaxxing essay, then The Claude Code Bible, then Codex in Production, and finish with Stake Your Claim so the work has an ownership model behind it.
